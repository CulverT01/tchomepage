                    Seki has a carpenter's plane and is using it to shave thin pieces of wood and when I say thin I mean translucent levels of thin. Its 'thickness' is 10 micrometres (0.001 millimetres), which for context, the diameter of a strand of human hair is about 80 micrometres. This is, however, to thick for Seki as he desires wood shavings that are 5 micrometres thick, so he continues to shave the block of wood and let the thick scraps float away onto Rumi's desk. Rumi tries to write on a 10 micrometre scrap, but it breaks, which makes sense when the standard thickness of paper is 50 micrometres, so she brushes them off her desk whilst being continually annoyed that Seki is causing a mess on her desk. Eventually, Seki shaves a scrap that looks to be that golden 5 micrometre (which is amazing, seeing as humans can at best see down to 100 micrometres from about 25cm away from the eye which Seki probably was to that scrap) but it floats onto Rumi's desk and in his haste to measure it, he hits Rumi's hand with his measuring device and she refuses to hand over the scrap (she slams a book down on top of it to deny Seki access to it) until he apologises for hitting her. He eventually apologises properly and Rumi lifts her book up, but the scrap has been ruined to shreds, which annoys Seki.

He then pulls a blueprint for another invention that will disrupt the pencil industry: Don't you hate it when pencils become too dull to use? Well at Seki Pencil Company (unofficially named and not real) we have the writing implement for you! for $10 a month we will rent you our new 'Combi-Pencil' that when pencil lead becomes too dull you can pull it off and you will have a brand new sharp lead ready to use! Ignore that such a device already exists on the market, known as a pop-a-point pencil. But Seki Pencil Company is not 1 trick pony either,as we have another design that solves a very common problem: sharpening pencils takes too long. We have invented a pencil that you can push the lead down the pencil so you only need to whittle the lead to resharpen it - very revolutionary and disruptive to the stale pencil market! We have named it the 'Speedy Sharp Pencil' and it will be available for $15 a month! Ignore the well established mechanical pencil which does the same thing only better as it requires no sharpening of the pencil. Our founder and Chief Product Officer, Toshinari Seki, will now demonstrate the 'Speedy Sharp Pencil' in action so you can see it effortless write like any other pencil until the lead snaps fairly on into usage. Well, teething troubles are to be expected with outside-the-pencil-box thinking, but I am sure this can be fixed by Seki and his lost motivation for anything, so we are filing for bankruptcy and closing down as Big Pencil couldn't handle the truth that their complacency was holding the industry back and needed to maintain its monopoly on the market.

Phantom Thief X is back up to his old tricks again as he has stolen the flute from Édouard Manet's (1832-1883) "The Fifer". Phantom Thief X isn't just interested in musical instruments or physical objects as he steals Mona Lisa's (Leonardo da Vinci's famous painting) smile before stealing the Rubin Vase, a bi-stable optical illusion created by Edgar Rubin (1886-1951), so all you get is a black square and no specifically shaped vase (the manga uses the word urn, which is incorrect regardless if the Japanese original used that word). Also Phantom Thief X is seen without his jacket as he has handed it to Venus in the "Birth of Venus" painting by Sandro Botticelli (1445-1510), to cover herself up. Phantom Thief has moved west and forward in time as he next found in Pablo Picasso's (1881-1973) "Guernica", although the Cubism gods don't take kindly to his free-spiritedness and have trapped him within the painting. Luckily for Phantom Thief X, Rumi comes to his rescue and draws him escaping the cubist ruins of Guernica, but Seki erases Rumi's efforts.
