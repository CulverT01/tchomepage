  <h1 class="col align-self-center">2026 Hankook London ePrix Race 2 Review</h1>
  <div class="row justify-content-center">
    <p class="col-8">
   This is my final race review of this season as we review race 2 of 2026 Hankook London ePrix. With season coming to a close, decisions regarding who is driving for what team are being made and some of these decisions have been made official. First: Theo Pourchaire will drive for the Opel GSE Formula E Team next year and is rumoured to be joined by Mitch Evans; Second: Envision Racing will have an entirely new line-up next year (first time in the team's history) with both Sebastien Buemi and Joel Eriksson leaving and the former moving into advisory and development role within the team. One of the likely replacements at Envision is Max Guenther and I think his supposed departure from DS Penske suggests to me that the Penske team will not race next season as no (unofficial or official) has been made if they are building their powertrain (which has never gone well for them) or partnering up with another manufacturer which would either be Mahindra or Lola-Yamaha. Envision will also have a new title sponsor next season as payments platform Paysafe will join the team.
    </p>

  <div class="row justify-content-center">
    <h2 class="row">Qualifying</h2>
    <p class="col-8">
    Group A consisted of: Pascal Wehrlein, Mitch Evans, Antonio Felix da Costa, Nick Cassidy, Nyck de Vries, Felipe Drugovich, Josep Maria 'Pepe' Marti, Joel Eriksson, Lucas Di Grassi, and Norman Nato. Lucas di Grassi does not compete in this session after his team decided to change his Inverter (MCU) after Free Practice 3, so will carry a 20-place grid penalty converted to 10-second stop-and-go penalty. He is also not the only one with a penalty or fail to set a representative time as Pepe has a 3-place grid penalty for his move on Jean-Eric Vergne last race and Norman would suffer brake issues on his out lap and which couldn't be resolved within the session so sets no lap time.<br/>
    Group B went next and consisted of Jake Dennis, Oliver Rowland, Edoardo Mortara, Nico Mueller, Sebastien Buemi, Dan Ticktum, Jean-Eric Vergne, Taylor Barnard, Max Guenther, and Zane Maloney. Like with Antonio last race, Edoardo clips the inside of turn 9 and bends his steering, but it doesn't hurt his pace too much. Also, Max will have a grid penalty after exiting pit lane under a red light, so will drop 3 places from where he will qualify.<br/>
    The 1st Quarter-Final was between Nick and Pascal, with the latter winning by 1.091 seconds thanks to Nick hitting the inside of turn 9, while the 2nd Quarter-Final was between Mitch and Nyck with the latter winning by 0.177 seconds. Quarter-Final 3 was between Dan and Nico, which saw the latter advance by a 23.599 seconds after Dan misses turn 10 due to Brake-By-Wire issues contributing to the mistake, while the final Quarter-Final was between Taylor and Edoardo which saw the latter moving on by 0.09 seconds. Semi-Final 1 was between Nyck and Pascal, which the former won by 0.022 seconds while Semi-Final 2 was between Nico and Edoardo, with the latter winning by 0.166 seconds. The Final saw Nyck winning by 0.195 seconds to take his 1st pole position in the Gen 3 era.
    </p>

    <h3 class="row">Nelson Piquet Jr Trophy</h3>
    <p class="col-8">
    The winner of the class is Nico Mueller, with 2nd going to Zane Maloney and 3rd to Felipe Drugovich. Pepe Marti leads from the standing start but is passed by Nico in Attack Mode into turn 1 on lap 4, who leads until the end. Despite winning the Nelson Piquet Jr Trophy like most would have expected at the start of the season and leading the standings for the entire season, you can't help but feel underwhelmed Nico's winning margin and class results because this shouldn't have been this close - he is the only trophy winner in Gen 3 not to seal the trophy before the final race. Nico had the best machinery and the most experience, but the fact that Felipe was 8 points off with 3 races to go suggests to me that his long term future a Porsche is not secure (he is warming the seat for Ayhancan Guven). Based off his performance this season, I expect Felipe to win the Trophy next season, although I will temper my expectations if Nissan's Gen 4 powertrain isn't one of the best next season.<br/>
