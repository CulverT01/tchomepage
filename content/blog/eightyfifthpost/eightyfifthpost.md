    <h2 class="row">Introduction</h2>
    <p class="col-8">
    Seeing prior to Monaco ePrix I played down Mitch's ability as Formula E driver, only for him to then lead the points standings for the most races consecutively in his career, I want to see if I could keep this jinks-ing ball running by seeing which competitors (driver, team, manufacturer) is likely to win their respective championships. This is done by calculating their average qualifying and finish to extrapolate the likely points total scored at each track for each competitor. The specific competitors we will look at are: Mitch Evans, Oliver Rowland, Edoardo Mortara, Pascal Wehrlein, Jaguar TCS Racing, Porsche Formula E Team, Porsche (manufacturer), and Jaguar (manufacturer). While some may consider 5th place Jake Dennis a title contender, I do not as I believe that he is too far back to make any gains on Mitch. The points for pole position and fastest lap will only be considered if a driver/team has the best record at a circuit amongst their competitors, for hypothetical positions, the average finishing position will be rounded to nearest whole number and we will only consider results from the Gen 3 era (this only affects London). For the teams, the average for race finishes will be calculated on car by car basis, while qualifying and fastest lap will see both results combined. For the manufacturers, the same will apply, but will only calculate the average for finishing position and by the grid which scores the manufacturer points.

    <h2 class="row">Alternative Methods</h2>
    <p class="col-8"> 
    As the points scored for finishing 11th is the same as finishing 20th, meaning that a DNF hurts more than a race where they are off-pace and not a true reflection of the competitive pace of a driver at that circuit. So, rather than relying on average qualifying, finishing, and fastest laps to calculate the possible points scored and instead we use average points scored then Mitch Evans would just win the championship with 178 points over Oliver Rowland's 175. This is because Mitch scores on average 8.75 points (rounded to 9) in Shanghai which is more than Oliver's average of 5.75 points (rounded to 6), obviously loses the lead in Tokyo as Oliver has an average points score of 22.33 (rounded to 22) compared to Mitch's 0, only to reclaim it in London as he has an average points score of 16.167 (rounded to 16) compared to Oliver's average of 6.25 (rounded to 6). Pascal is good at the upcoming circuits, but is not the greatest, so slowly falls out of contention and Edoardo's only great track comping up in terms of results is London, but his average points score there is 4.67 (rounded to 5) so he falls out of title contention a lot faster and a lot harder (I don't think he will finish 4th in the standings come the end of the season).<br/>
    For the teams there's no real change as Jaguar TCS Racing would still win 330 to 292 as Porsche Formula E Team can only outscore them in Tokyo in terms of average points but its not be a large amount as Jaguar scores an average of 4 points compared to Porsche's average of 15. For the manufacturers Porsche still win the title with 474 points to Jaguar's 433 and the gap is slightly closer because across the 13 races (4 Shanghai races, 3 Tokyo races, and 6 London races) I looked at, a Jaguar powertrain was victorious 6 times so the points for 1st cover for the positions with less points that the finish does not.
    </p>

   <div class="row justify-content-center">
    <h2 class="row">Conclusion</h2>
    <p class="col-8"> 
    Based of average finishes, qualifying, and fastest race laps Mitch Evans win his title and does so based on average points scored as well, however, he would win the championship under either method with the lowest points total since Nyck de Vries's title back in Season 7 (2020-2021). Jaguar TCS Racing would win the their 2nd ever Teams' title, more convincingly based on average finishes, qualifying, and fastest race laps compared to average points scored. Porsche would win the Manufacturers' title under both methods again, with average points scored providing a slightly closer battle. Ultimately, this is no real indicator as to what will happen as the data sets are quite small and can be influenced by outliers as for example: Oliver Rowland has on average scored 6.25 points across his 4 races in London in Gen 3, but all of them came in 1 race and his other finishes are outside the top 10 (next best finish is an 11th). Another example is that of Edoardo Mortara, whose 2 DNFs in London hurt the average finish (11.67) of someone who is likely to be guaranteed a points finish as shown by his average points score of 4.67.
