  <h1 class="col align-self-center">2026 Hankook London ePrix Race 1 Review</h1>
  <div class="row justify-content-center">
    <p class="col-8">
   Hello and welcome to my review of race 1 of 2026 Hankook London ePrix, the penultimate race of this season and final PitBoost race of the season as well as Gen 3. There are various pieces of new that broke since Tokyo, for both the sport and the drivers in it and this post will focus on the pieces about Formula E and the tomorrow's will focus on the driver news instead. First, the Monaco ePrix doubleheader next season will move to 1st-2nd May 2027 rather than its previous date of 15-16th May as there are events going on in the country on the original weekend, also there is no longer a clash with the World Endurance Championship race at Spa (happy Sebastien Buemi noises). Also Formula E and ACM (Automobile Club de Monaco), the National Motorsport Authority in charge of motorsport events in Monaco, announce that the Monaco ePrix will be on the calendar until 2031 (Season 17 or 1st season of Gen 5). From next season, Disney+ will be the global streaming home for Formula E in a multi-year agreement which will exist alongside current free-to-air agreements.<br/>
   There are few teams with special liveries this weekend as Porsche Formula E Team are running a tribute to their time being sponsored by Rothmans tobacco back in the early-to-mid 1980s, a time period in which they won 4 24 hours of Le Mans (1982-83 and 1986-87) and 1986 Paris-Dakar Rally. Lucas di Grassi has a multicoloured car as his livery for this weekend takes a small bit of inspiration from the liveries of all the previous seasons he has competed in. DS Penske have inverted their colours for this ePrix, so what was black is now gold and vice-versa.
    </p>

    <h2 class="row">Qualifying</h2>
    <p class="col-8">
    The Free Practice sessions saw very incidents as Cupra Kiro would initially send Dan Ticktum out in Free Practice 1 with a loose front-right tyre and he had to go around the entire circuit very slowly. In Free Practice 2 Oliver Rowland would clip the wall in turn and damaging his steering, but it was repairable within the session. Also, Nico Mueller would receive a Black-and-White flag for weaving during the session, while Felipe Drugovich would receive the same flag after he made contact with Pascal Wehrlein, which infuriated the German.<br/>
    Group A consisted of: Jake Dennis, Pascal Wehrlein, Edoardo Mortara, Nick Cassidy, Nyck de Vries, Felipe Drugovich, Jean-Eric Vergne, Dan Ticktum, Lucas Di Grassi, and Norman Nato. Edoardo has 5-place grid penalty for the incident last race and Lucas has a 3-place penalty for speeding under Red Flag conditions. Jake and Nyck thought it was already the race with the moves were throwing against each other as Jake would attempt an overtake in turn 19 which resulted in contact and Nyck would defend aggressively into turn 10 after impeding Jake for entire lap after Jake's move. Both get away with this without penalty. The top 4 in group A and thus made it to the Duel stage were: Pascal, Dan, Jake, and Jean-Eric.<br/>
    Group B went next and consisted of Mitch Evans, Oliver Rowland, Antonio Felix da Costa, Nico Mueller, Sebastien Buemi, Josep Maria 'Pepe' Marti, Joel Eriksson, Taylor Barnard, Max Guenther, and Zane Maloney. Antonio would clip the wall on the inside of turn 9 and damage his steering, but would continue unlike Max Guenther who would hit the wall on his first build lap with his rear-left wheel and incur suspension damage that was significant enough for DS Penske not send him back out after looking at it. The 4 that made it into Duel stage from group B were: Nico, Mitch, Joel, and Antonio.<br/>
    The 1st Quarter-Final was between Jake and Dan, with the latter winning by 0.072 seconds while the 2nd Quarter-Final was between Jean-Eric and Pascal with the latter winning by 0.354 seconds. Quarter-Final 3 was between Joel and Mitch which saw the latter advance by a 0.388 seconds, while the final Quarter-Final was between Antonio and Nico which saw the latter moving on by 0.174 seconds after Antonio made a mistake through turn 16. Semi-Final 1 was between Dan and Pascal, which the latter won by 2.679 seconds thanks to manufacturer orders (something that Pascal didn't want his rivals to do), while Semi-Final 2 was between Mitch and Nico, with the latter winning by 0.250 seconds. The Final saw Pascal winning by 1.143 seconds thanks to manufacturer orders, although these kind of shenanigans will be clamped down next season.
    </p>

    <h2 class="row">Race</h2>
    <h3 class="row">Overall Drivers' Championship</h3>
    <p class="col-8"> 
    Pascal Wehrlein wins the ePrix from Jake Dennis in 2nd and Antonio Felix da Costa in 3rd. The race winner got lucky because if Lucas di Grassi doesn't suffer his technical issue ("the engine kept pushing") on lap 21 then Pascal is undercut by Mitch Evans and his teammate (although Nico Mueller would have let him through anyway) and he finishes at best 2nd. Also, for someone who was wary of his rivals trying manipulative tactics, he sure benefitted from having his teammate do his best Season 9 Sebastien Buemi impression. If you are wondering what his defence to this situation it is this: <i>Well, others did it - Jaguar did it in Monaco back in Season 10</i>. Pascal, that is not a defence and certainly not one that will net you any moral high ground as in fact it probably makes you look like a hypocrite. Lucas was not the only retirement this race as Zane Maloney would have his rear-right suspension fail (he suspected driveshaft in the moment) on lap and on lap 38, Pepe Marti makes a move on the inside of Jean-Eric in turn 16, the 2 make contact and both retire in the pits then the stewards would find Pepe at fault and it is their report that I base my description on as we never see it (thanks Tv!). Edoardo Mortara tries to make it 5 retirements as he goes for an optimistic move on Dan Ticktum into turn 1 on lap 37 and pushes the Cupra Kiro driver wide and picks a 5-second time penalty post-race from the stewards and thus finishes behind Dan, who still loses positions as Edoardo's move allows Taylor Barnard to slip past Dan.
