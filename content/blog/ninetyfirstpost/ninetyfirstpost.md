                <h3 class="row">Alternative Format</h3>
                    <div> 
                    For the Overall Drivers' Championship, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is after race 2 of the Jakarta ePrix, the 11th race of the season. The leader at this point is Pascal Wehrlein, who leads with 138 points, but the eventual winner is Jake Dennis who wins the championship during qualifying of the final race and with a points total of 218 and he was 3rd at the point of Mathematical ineligibility with 130 points. The differing factor is sheer results as Jake would finish 4 of the last 5 races on the podium (the other finish is 4th) while the best finish for Pascal in that same span is 7th.<br/>
                    For the Customer Trophy for Drivers Championship, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is after the Monaco ePrix, the 9th race of the season. The leader at this point is Nick Cassidy with 151 points, but he doesn't go on to win the championship as instead Jake Dennis takes it instead with 255 points and was 3rd at this point with 124 points. Jake's success is down to great results from him (finishes no lower than 2nd in class in the last 7 races) and bad results from Nick (Rome 2 and London 1).<br/>
                    For the Nelson Piquet Jr Trophy Championship, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is after race 1 of the Jakarta ePrix, the 10th race of the season. The leader at this point is Sergio Sette Camara, who leads with 153 points and has mathematically eliminated Roberto Mehri, who had just made his first start this season in this race. Sergio, would not, however, defend this lead as: a DNS in the next race (Jakarta race 2), a DNF in race 2 of the Rome ePrix (battery voltage issue), and DSQ from race 1 of the London ePrix (failure to change a damaged front wing when instructed to do so by a safety official). This means that the eventual winner of the trophy is Dan Ticktum who seals the championship with a race to spare and a final points total of 242, despite being 3rd at the point of Mathematical ineligibility with 132 points.<br/>
                    For the Overall Teams' Championship, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is also after race 2 of the Jakarta ePrix, with TAG Heuer Porsche Formula E Team leading the standings with 225 points. They do not not win the Overall Teams' Championship this season as instead it goes to Envision Racing who were 2nd at the point of Mathematical ineligibility with 207 points. Envision' winning points total is 311 and their victory is down to better results as despite 2 DNFs for their star driver Nick Cassidy, they win twice in the final 5 races while Porsche's best finish in that time frame is a singular 3rd in Portland.<br/>
                    For the Customer Teams' Championship, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is also after the Monaco ePrix, with Envision Racing leading at this point with 163 points and they would defend this lead and win the championship with 291 points.<br/>
                    For the Manufacturers' Cup, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is also after race 1 of the Jakarta ePrix, with Stellantis leading the standings at this point with 262 points and they would go on to defend this lead with final score of 422 as well as sealing the championship with a race remaining.
                    </div>
                <h3 class="row">Real Life</h3>
                    <div>
                    For the Drivers' Championship, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is after the Portland ePrix, the 12th race of the season. The leader at this point is Jake Dennis, who leads the standings with 154 points and he would go on to win this championship with a race to spare and a final points total of 229 points.<br/>
                    For the Teams' Championship, the point of Mathematical ineligibility is also after the Portland ePrix, with TAG Heuer Porsche Formula E Team leading the standings with 231 points, however, they would not go on to win the championship as instead Envision Racing, who 2nd with 225 points at the point of Mathematical ineligibility, would win the championship with 304 points. Envision have a car on the podium in 3 of the final 4 races and only thrice does a Envision car fail to score points (Sebastien Buemi DNFs from Rome 1 and Nick Cassidy finishes 14th in Rome 2 and DNFs in London 1), while Porsche's best finish is 7th from Pascal and Antonio doesn't score any points in those final 4 races.<br/>
                    For the hypothetical Manufacturers' Cup, the point of Mathematical ineligibility would have been after race 1 of the Jakarta ePrix, the 10th race of the season. The leader at this point is Porsche with 314 points however, they would not defend this lead as Jaguar would have come from behind to win the title with 491 points. This is despite them being 2nd with 284 points at the point of Mathematical ineligibility and their success is down to them having 3 1-2 manufacturer finishes in the last 6 races while Porsche best points total is a manufacturer 2-3 in Portland.
